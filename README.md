structural materials, surveying, and GIS. Maintained by
[Syed Rafi Ahmed](https://github.com/SyedRafiAhmedGIS). Every link below was opened and
verified in October 2026, and every description is written in my own words rather than copied
from the resource or from any other list.

## Structural Materials and Concrete

- [ASTM International](https://www.astm.org) — the US standards body behind ASTM C143 (slump)
  and C39 (compressive strength); students should read the free official summaries of each
  test method before performing it.
- [American Concrete Institute](https://www.concrete.org) — global home of ACI codes,
  reports, and certification; its publication previews connect laboratory testing practice to
  specification writing.
- [American Cement Association](https://www.cement.org) — research and education body for
  North American cement manufacturers (formerly the Portland Cement Association), with free
  technical publications on cement types and durability.
- [OpenSees](https://opensees.berkeley.edu) — open-source finite-element framework from the
  Pacific Earthquake Engineering Research Center; the free path beyond spreadsheet structural
  analysis for advanced students.
- [SkyCiv](https://skyciv.com) — browser-based structural analysis with a permanent free tier,
  so every student can verify one frame problem without a licence barrier.

## Surveying and Geodesy

- [NCEES](https://ncees.org) — the US licensing body for engineers and surveyors; its
  Fundamentals of Surveying exam specification shows students why each syllabus topic matters.
- [International Federation of Surveyors (FIG)](https://www.fig.net) — global federation of
  surveying associations with free commission publications on practice and standards.
- [NOAA National Geodetic Survey](https://geodesy.noaa.gov) — the United States authority for
  the national coordinate system, with survey control markers whose datasheets make abstract
  datums concrete.
- [U.S. Geological Survey](https://www.usgs.gov) — federal portal for topographic maps,
  elevation models, and earth-science data.
- [Khan Academy — Geometry](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/geometry) — self-paced triangle
  and coordinate geometry, the direct prerequisite for traverse and curve computations.
- [Khan Academy — Statistics and Probability](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/statistics-probability) —
  sixteen units covering data display and testing; the background needed for control charts
  and characteristic-strength calculations.

## GIS and Remote Sensing Software

- [QGIS](https://qgis.org) — the open-source desktop GIS that behaves identically on Windows,
  macOS, and Linux, making it the dependable choice for a whole lab cohort.
- [QGIS Training Manual](https://docs.qgis.org/latest/en/docs/training_manual/) — the official
  step-by-step tutorial with worked exercises from data import through to map layout.
- [QGIS Documentation](https://docs.qgis.org/latest/en/docs/) — the full user guide and API
  reference for the current QGIS release.
- [GRASS GIS](https://grass.osgeo.org) — veteran open-source suite with strong raster and
  terrain geoprocessing modules.
- [PostGIS](https://postgis.net) — spatial extension for PostgreSQL that turns plain SQL into
  a spatial query engine.
- [GeoServer](https://geoserver.org) — open-source server for publishing layers as WMS and
  WFS map services.
- [GeoPandas](https://geopandas.org) — pandas extended to spatial objects; the natural first
  step into scripted geospatial analysis.
- [Leaflet](https://leafletjs.com) — lightweight open-source JavaScript library for
  interactive web maps.
- [Google Earth Engine](https://earthengine.google.com) — cloud platform for planetary-scale
  satellite data analysis, free for research and education.

## Open Datasets

- [USGS EarthExplorer](https://earthexplorer.usgs.gov) — download portal for the Landsat
  archive, aerial photography, and elevation products, searchable by map or place name.
- [Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu) — open access to
  Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 ten-metre optical imagery of Europe's eyes on Earth.
- [NASA Earthdata](https://earthdata.nasa.gov) — NASA's catalogue of earth-observation data
  across atmosphere, ocean, and land missions. *(Blocks automated checkers; opens normally in
  a browser — verify manually.)*
- [National Remote Sensing Centre (NRSC / ISRO)](https://www.nrsc.gov.in) — India's earth
  observation centre, with satellite products and thematic services for South Asia.
- [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org) — the free, community-built world map whose
  raw data is reusable under the Open Database License.
- [Geofabrik extracts](https://download.geofabrik.de) — regularly updated OpenStreetMap data
  split by country and region, ideal for offline classroom work.
- [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com) — free public-domain vector and raster map
  data at 1:10m, 1:50m, and 1:110m scales.
- [GADM](https://gadm.org) — consistently built administrative boundaries for all countries,
  down to district and sub-district level.
- [OpenTopography](https://opentopography.org) — high-resolution lidar topography and standard
  DEMs with convenient processing tools.
- [World Bank Open Data](https://data.worldbank.org) — free country-level development
  indicators, perfect for a first attribute-join exercise.

## Python and Notebook Tools

- [Python](https://www.python.org) — the readable language in which engineering analysis is
  best taught.
- [NumPy](https://numpy.org) — array computation and the engine behind interpolated surfaces.
- [pandas](https://pandas.pydata.org) — tabular analysis that bridges from spreadsheets to
  scripts.
- [Matplotlib](https://matplotlib.org) — plotting library for every chart a lab report needs.
- [Shapely](https://shapely.readthedocs.io) — exact geometry operations: buffers,
  intersections, unions.
- [Rasterio](https://rasterio.readthedocs.io) — reading and writing geospatial rasters in
  Python.
- [Folium](https://python-visualization.github.io/folium) — Python bindings for Leaflet, one
  import away from an interactive map.
- [Jupyter](https://jupyter.org) — the notebook environment that mixes narrative and
  executable code.
- [Google Colab](https://colab.research.google.com) — free hosted notebooks that run with zero
  installation, ideal for classroom-only settings.

## Learning Platforms

- [MIT OpenCourseWare — Civil and Environmental Engineering](https://ocw.mit.edu/search/?d=Civil%20and%20Environmental%20Engineering) —
  over one hundred complete MIT courses with notes and assignments, free to browse.
- [NPTEL](https://nptel.ac.in) — India's national platform of institute-recorded video courses
  across engineering, including civil engineering offerings.
- [Practical Engineering (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@PracticalEngineeringChannel) —
  physical-model demonstrations of infrastructure and materials behaviour.
